The core loop is strong: risk, reward and a “dash to the exit” tension, with speed upgrades feeding directly into it. It’s a good blend of the Keyboard Escape progression and a collect-and-escape game.

Thoughts

Pets need a purpose. Right now they’re the goal but not the payoff. Having them boost speed, bag size or income would loop them back into progression.
The house needs a reason to exist. If it’s purely cosmetic, it’ll feel disconnected unless pets live there or it offers buffs or showcase value.
Paid areas are fine, but keep a free route. Gamepass-only access works, but players tend to accept it better if non-payers can unlock the same areas later through rebirths.
Mix-and-match parts is your hook. It needs standardised attachment points on every part (head, body, legs, tail and so on) so any combination looks right. I’d settle that early.
A 3-item bag is tight. That’s good for tension, but it needs bag upgrades (a strong monetisation and progression lever) or runs will feel too slow.

Comparing currency systems across similar games for context.

How similar games handle currency: most use two tiers.

Soft currency (coins): earned by selling things you collect, spent on progression like bag size, house items, speed upgrades and pet building fees.
Premium (Robux): pays for gamepasses (area access, 2x speed, extra bag slots) and sometimes skips or boosts.

Here, selling spare parts for coins fits well, and since duplicates are expected, it gives them a use. Rebirth is a third, non-spendable layer that just multiplies your speed.

Your answers

Part stats: a pet’s stats become the sum of its parts. I’d keep it to 3-4 stats, such as speed, bag capacity, coin bonus and luck, so pets feed back into the loop.
Trading: great for engagement, but it’s the biggest exploit risk. Gate it behind a minimum level or account age and log every trade server-side. It also needs rules so players can’t buy past your paid areas.
Maze return: a maze makes the chase tense, but a long walk back after every catch will frustrate players. A short “respawn at the entrance” with a rebuild-the-route feel works better than a long trek.
Teleport-out points: good, they work as extraction points. Spacing them out is your main difficulty lever.
Shared servers: to avoid griefing, I’d have each player get their own chaser but share the part spawns, so you still compete for parts without being blocked by the creature.
Audience: Roblox skews heavily towards mobile, so I’d assume it and keep controls and UI simple.

Weighing a pay-to-win concern in club design request.

Five coin clubs and two paid ones works for the count. The longer reach on the paid ones is the part I’d push back on, because it’s real pay-to-win. Every hit steals a bag item, and the whole game is about keeping your items, so a reach advantage lets paying players take from others more easily. In a PvP setup that’s the kind of thing that makes free players quit or complain.

Options, from safest to closest to what you asked

Same reach, better looks (my pick): the two paid clubs have the same stats as your top coin club. They get a unique skin, swing effect and hit sound instead.
Sidegrade: the paid clubs reach slightly further but swing slower or have a longer cooldown. Reach isn’t strictly better, so it’s a choice rather than a free upgrade.
Longer reach as you described: this works if the gap is small and a top coin club comes close. Players can then catch up by playing, and the paid ones remain a convenience.

I’d go with option 1 or 2. If you choose option 3, I’d keep the paid reach to about 10% above the best coin club and track hit rates by club type in monitoring, so we can spot it if paying players dominate.
